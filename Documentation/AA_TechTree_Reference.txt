MOD OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Archer Aerospace is a full replacement technology tree for Kerbal Space Program designed around capability-driven progression, historical inspiration, and player choice.

Rather than a single linear unlock path, the tree is organized into parallel, independently investable branches. Core systems unlock early and complete early, while infrastructure-heavy and speculative systems are intentionally delayed or sparse.

This project prioritizes:
- Clarity over part spam
- Capability unlocks over arbitrary gating
- Long-term gameplay feel over strict historical simulation

This is a personal, single-player mod and is not intended for public release or stock balance parity.

================================================================================
2. HIGH-LEVEL DESIGN GOALS

Key goals guiding all tech tree decisions:

- Aircraft-first progression, with aircraft remaining relevant into late game
- A core, faction-agnostic rocketry spine that always advances
- Optional crewed program overlays layered on top of the core
- Support branches that unlock new mission capabilities, not raw scaling
- Intentional late-game restraint (T9–T10 are sparse by design)

================================================================================
3. TECH ERA TIMELINE (ABSTRACTED)

Tech tiers loosely correspond to real-world aerospace eras. These mappings are approximate and intentionally abstracted.

T0 — Early Jet & Experimental Flight (1930s–1940s)
	> Early jet engines, experimental aircraft, no spaceflight

T1 — Postwar Aviation & Early Research (mid-1940s)
	> Improved aircraft design, basic electronics, early science

T2 — Sounding Rockets & Pre-Orbital Research (late 1940s–mid-1950s)
	> Suborbital rockets, atmospheric research, early probes

T2.5 — Sputnik Era (1957)
	> First artificial satellites, minimal orbital capability

T3 — Early Orbital Capability (late 1950s–early 1960s)
	> Reliable orbit, early recovery systems

T3.5 — Vostok Era (early 1960s)
	> First human spaceflight, single-crew capsules

T4 — Mercury / Early Space Operations (early–mid 1960s)
	> Multi-orbit missions, docking foundations

T4.5 — Voskhod & Early Probe Expansion (mid-1960s)
	> EVA capability, early probe buses

T5 — Gemini / Rendezvous & Deep Space (mid–late 1960s)
	> Docking becomes routine, long-duration missions

T5.5 — Soyuz / Operational Crewed Spaceflight (late 1960s–1970s)
	> Standardized crew rotation and orbital operations

T6 — Apollo / Heavy Lift Era (late 1960s–early 1970s)
	> Lunar missions, very large launch vehicles

T6.5 — Salyut / Early Space Stations (1970s–1980s)
	> Long-duration habitation and orbital infrastructure

T7 — Shuttle / Reusability Era (1980s–2010s)
	> Large payload delivery, partial reusability

T8 — ISS / Modern Orbital Infrastructure (1990s–Present)
	> Sustained multinational orbital presence

T9 — Near-Future Operations
	> High autonomy, advanced guidance systems

T10 — Speculative / Post-Real-World Technology
	> Experimental propulsion and infrastructure (reserved)

13. — TECH TREE NODE POSITIONING & LAYOUT GUIDELINES

This section explains how technology nodes are positioned within the KSP R&D view. These rules are intended to keep the tree readable, consistent, and easy to extend.

NODE POSITION FORMAT
pos = X,Y,Z

X-AXIS — TIER PROGRESSION (HORIZONTAL)

T1.5	-2300		Intermediate convergence
T2	-2200		Early expansion

T3	-2000		Early orbital capability

Notes:
- Values may shift slightly for readability.
- Half-tier nodes should sit visually between full tiers.
- Parking is intentionally far right for easy discovery.

Y-AXIS — BRANCH LANES (VERTICAL)

The Y-axis separates branches into horizontal “lanes.”
Lower Y values appear higher on screen (inverted axis).

These are soft bands, not exact requirements.

UPPER / SCIENCE AREA
	Y ≈ 900		Science & scanning
	Y ≈ 950		Probe cores / guidance
	Y ≈ 1000	Electronics & power

MIDDLE AREA
	Y ≈ 1100	Structural & manufacturing
	Y ≈ 1199	Aircraft (primary starting lane)
	Y ≈ 1200	Archer Aerospace rocketry

LOWER / HEAVY SYSTEMS
	Y ≈ 1250–1300	NASA crewed programs
	Y ≈ 1350	Soviet crewed programs
	Y ≈ 1450–1500	Recovery & separation
	Y ≈ 1550	SRB progression
	
Z-AXIS (LAYERING / DRAW ORDER)

The Z value controls draw order only, using negative numbers.
Higher Z values are rendered above lower Z values and above
dependency lines.

Z decreases monotonically with tier depth (more negative = renders on top).

Standard mapping:
	T0		Z = 0
	T1		Z = -1
	T1.5		Z = -1.5
	T2		Z = -2
	T2.5		Z = -2.5
	T3		Z = -3
	T3.5		Z = -3.5
	T4		Z = -4
	T4.5		Z = -4.5
	T5		Z = -5
	T5.5		Z = -5.5
	T6		Z = -6
	T6.5		Z = -6.5
	T7		Z = -7
	T7.5		Z = -7.5
	T8		Z = -8
	T9		Z = -9
	T10		Z = -10
	T99		Z = -99

The Z axis must never be used to imply progression,
prerequisites, or balance.

Notes:
- Branches should stay vertically consistent across tiers.
- NASA and Soviet lines are intentionally below the core Archer spine.
- Support branches sit above core flight systems.
- Visual clarity takes precedence over strict coordinates.

DESIGN INTENT
- Horizontal movement answers “when does this exist?”
- Vertical placement answers “what kind of capability is this?”
- Parallel branches should feel parallel on screen.
- New branches should claim a vertical lane early and stay in it.

14. TECH NODE COSTS — DEVELOPMENT STATUS (LOCKED PROCESS)

All technology node science costs in the Archer Aerospace tech tree are temporary placeholders during active development.

CURRENT POLICY

All node science costs are non-final

Costs do not represent balance, pacing, or intended progression

Costs may be inconsistent across tiers and branches

No design intent should be inferred from current values

For development and iteration purposes, all nodes should be treated as having an effective cost of 1.

RATIONALE

During tech tree construction, node costs create noise and friction that obscure the real work:

Branch layout and visual clarity

Part placement correctness

Progression logic and dependency flow

Identification of missing or misassigned parts

Locking in costs too early makes refactors more expensive and encourages premature balance decisions.

FUTURE FINALIZATION (DEFERRED)

Science costs will be finalized only after the following are complete:

All branches and nodes are stable

Part assignments are audited and complete

No orphaned or parking-node parts remain

Gameplay testing validates intended progression feel

Final costs will be applied globally and intentionally, using relative weighting between branches rather than strict tier-based scaling.

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

During development, nodes may be set to:

cost = 1


Temporary cost variance is acceptable but discouraged

No tuning or balancing work should be done until explicitly scheduled

DESIGN INTENT

Node cost tuning is a polish phase, not a construction phase.
Structure comes first. Balance comes last.

16. MOD DEPENDENCY & SCOPE ASSUMPTIONS (LOCKED)

Archer Aerospace is designed as a personal, single-player tech tree with explicit assumptions about part availability.

DESIGN ASSUMPTION

The tech tree defines the parts it supports

All required parts are expected to exist in the environment

The tree is not intended for third-party distribution at this time

IMPLICATIONS

No mod dependency checks are required

No conditional MM logic for missing mods is needed

Nodes may safely reference parts without fallback handling

Simplicity and determinism are prioritized over portability

DESIGN INTENT

This project optimizes for:

Fast iteration

Clear intent

Minimal defensive logic

If public release or redistribution is ever considered, dependency handling may be revisited as a separate design phase.
